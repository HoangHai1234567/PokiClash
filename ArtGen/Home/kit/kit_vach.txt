Four separate cliff-wall pieces for an isometric mobile base-building game map, on a plain flat pure white background. Each piece is the vertical earth cliff face at the edge of a raised grassy plateau, seen from the same high isometric angle as the blocks in image 2 (2:1 isometric: the top edge of each wall runs diagonally down to the right at about 27 degrees, the sides of the face are vertical).

FRAME (most important): follow image 1 exactly. In each slot the tan parallelogram is the cliff face and the thin green strip along its top edge is the grassy rim of the plateau above it. Draw the cliff face filling exactly the tan shape: same position, same slant, same height. The green strip becomes a hand-drawn grassy lip with small tufts hanging slightly over the top of the cliff. The bottom edge of the face meets the ground with a few small grass tufts and pebbles at the foot. Grey dashed lines mean the wall continues on that side: there the face must run straight to the edge of the tan shape and stop with a plain clean vertical cut, no rocks, no bush, no end decoration, so that copies of the pieces join side by side without a visible seam.

- Slot 2: straight cliff segment, a variant with a few roots and two or three embedded stones.
- Slot 3: the cliff comes from the left and ENDS on the right: the light grey area in image 1 is where the cliff slopes down into a pile of mossy rocks and a small round bush reaching the ground.
- Slot 4: the mirror of slot 3: the cliff comes from the right and ends on the left the same way.
Above the green rim: nothing, plain white (no trees, no plateau top surface). Below the foot: plain white. Do not draw the numbers, the grey frame lines or the dashed lines.

STYLE: exactly the cliffs of image 2, drawn like image 3: hand-drawn, wobbly dark-brown ink outlines, warm orange-brown earth with vertical crayon striations and a few horizontal layer lines, bright green grassy lip, coloured pencil and wax crayon texture, one flat colour per area.
